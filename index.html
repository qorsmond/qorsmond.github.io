Quintin Orsmond

Building software for over 25 years, and still not bored of it.

Hi, I’m Quintin. I’m mostly self-taught, and most of what I know I learnt by building things and living with the consequences.

I’ve never stayed in one lane for long. Over the years I’ve built GIS systems for farmers and silo managers, desktop apps that kept working when the network didn’t, web apps, mobile apps, APIs and the databases behind them, and an online framing shop I wrote from scratch because none of the off-the-shelf options quite fit. I’ve designed the architecture, picked stacks I then had to live with, and led the teams building on them.

I’ve seen plenty of languages, frameworks, methodologies and this-changes-everything moments come and go. A few genuinely changed how we work. What strikes me is how little the hard part has moved: working out what people actually need before we build it, and keeping the result understandable for whoever has to change it next. Quite often that’s me, a few months later, wondering what I was thinking.

Lately most of my attention goes to what coding agents are doing to the craft. I love how fast they are. I’m less keen on how easy they’ve made it to build the wrong thing quickly, so I’ve been working on ways to keep the thinking connected to the doing.

So why am I still at it after all these years? Because I still love building things. The part I enjoy most has just moved: it’s less the typing now, and more working out what’s actually worth building. Looking back, that was always the fun part.

Where the years went

I could give you a wall of logos (this site used to), but it wouldn’t tell you much. Here’s roughly where my time has gone instead.

Frontend

From wrestling IE9 to AngularJS, Angular, React, Vue and Web Components, and lately design systems. Frameworks come and go. I’ve always cared more about the fundamentals and patterns underneath them, because those are the bits you get to keep.

Backend and data

Mostly .NET, from ASP.NET Web API through to ASP.NET Core, with Node.js where it made more sense. SQL Server, SQLite and MariaDB underneath, Dapper or EF Core in between, and a fair bit of reporting in SSRS.

GIS

Years of geographic systems for agriculture at Farm-IT: MapGuide, spatial data in SQL Server, GeoServer and OpenLayers, for agronomists managing budgets and silo managers running their daily operations.

Mobile and offline

Production apps in Flutter, React Native and Ionic, and Cordova before those. I was building web apps that worked offline back in 2012, a few years before anyone called them progressive web apps.

Testing and delivery

I was pointing people at behaviour-driven development back in 2011 and have leaned on tests ever since: xUnit and SpecFlow, Jest, Puppeteer and Cucumber, with Jenkins doing the shipping. Done means proven, not claimed.

Architecture and leading teams

I learnt early that choosing a stack is a big responsibility, because it shapes everything that comes after. I’ve made those calls, lived with the results, and led teams through them. Less is more, nearly every time: less for the user to think about, and less for the next developer to untangle.

AI-assisted engineering

The newest chapter. Agents have changed how quickly we can build, so I’m working out what that means for everything around the typing: how much process an agent needs, which code deserves a careful read, and how to keep a codebase understandable when it grows this fast.
